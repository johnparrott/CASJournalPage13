Description

The Curiosity Journal is a space for you to explore questions that arise as you learn about child and adolescent development. After reading or listening to Chapter 13, you will identify something that made you curious, turn that curiosity into a focused question, investigate the question using credible sources, and develop an initial answer based on what you discover.

Your question does not need to have one simple or final answer. The goal is to move beyond recalling information from the chapter and begin thinking like a developmentalist: asking meaningful questions, examining evidence, and using what you find to build a thoughtful explanation.

For this entry, your question should connect to one or more ideas from Chapter 13, such as the principles of development, periods of child and adolescent development, major issues in development, developmental research methods, theories of child development, or the different contexts that shape development.

Rubric

Gold

Excellent

  • Curiosity Question
    The question is clear, focused, genuinely investigable, and meaningfully connected to Chapter 13.
    4 points
  • Source Selection
    Both sources are credible, relevant, and well chosen for investigating the question. Together, they contribute useful evidence or perspectives.
    4 points
  • Explanation of Sources
    Clearly and accurately explains the important information, evidence, or perspective contributed by each source.
    4 points
  • Initial Answer and Synthesis
    Directly answers the question and thoughtfully brings together ideas from the chapter and both sources. The reasoning is clear and supported by evidence.
    6 points
  • Clarity and Communication
    The entry is organized, understandable, and written primarily in the student’s own words. Sources and ideas are represented accurately.
    2 points

Silver

Developing

  • Curiosity Question
    The question connects to the chapter but may be broad, unclear, or answerable with a simple fact.
    3 points
  • Source Selection
    The sources are generally relevant, but one may have limited credibility, depth, or usefulness.
    3 points
  • Explanation of Sources
    Explains what the sources say, but the discussion may be brief, general, or mostly summary.
    3 points
  • Initial Answer and Synthesis
    Provides an answer and uses evidence, but the ideas may remain somewhat separate, underdeveloped, or only partially connected to the question.
    5 points
  • Clarity and Communication
    The main ideas are understandable, but parts of the entry may be unclear, repetitive, or too dependent on source wording.
    1 point

Bronze

Needs Improvement

  • Curiosity Question
    The question has a limited connection to Chapter 13 or does not provide a useful direction for investigation.
    2 points
  • Source Selection
    The sources have limited relevance, credibility, or value for answering the question.
    2 points
  • Explanation of Sources
    Provides limited or unclear explanation of how the sources contribute to the investigation.
    2 points
  • Initial Answer and Synthesis
    Provides a limited answer, relies heavily on unsupported opinion, or makes little use of the chapter and sources together.
    3 points
  • Clarity and Communication
    The writing is difficult to follow or does not clearly communicate the student’s reasoning.
    1 point
